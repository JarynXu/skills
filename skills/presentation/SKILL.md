# Presentation

Help the intended audience understand something consequential through a sequence the presenter can actually deliver, or the reader can follow alone. Slides serve that communication; attractive pages and a successfully saved file are not sufficient outcomes.

## Fit the work to its use

Establish the audience's starting knowledge, intended understanding or decision, delivery mode, time budget and requested artifacts from the brief and existing materials. Distinguish live talks, standalone reading and facilitated training; do not invent discussion, exercises or assignments for a lecture. Support an inexperienced presenter with usable explanations and transitions rather than more text on screen.

Read the actual source and neighboring slides before revising an existing deck. Preserve the user's language, approved meaning, visual identity and unrelated work. Critique is not permission to edit; a local repair is not permission to rebuild the talk. Resolve missing information that changes the outcome; otherwise use explicit, reversible assumptions without requiring a questionnaire or approval at every stage.

## Establish meaning before allocating pages

Identify the central takeaway and the claims, explanations or evidence needed to reach it. Order material by the audience's prerequisites and questions, not the source document's headings. Remove irrelevant material; introduce necessary concepts before relying on them. A narrative can explain, compare, demonstrate or support a decision without forcing every talk into a dramatic story.

Trace consequential factual claims and data to applicable evidence. Inspect project sources, original external guidance or a small experiment according to the uncertainty; do not research familiar material by ritual. Keep facts, assumptions and illustrative examples distinct. Preserve qualifications, units, denominators and time periods; do not invent statistics, quotations, case identifiers or production experience to make the presentation persuasive. Keep useful source attribution with the claim or in a clearly mapped source note.

Write or refine the explanation before deriving slides when that helps the task, especially when a script is requested. Do not merely split prose across pages or require a separate manuscript for every deck. Allocate time to explanations, examples and transitions; page count alone does not establish duration.

## Give each slide a communication job

Before polishing a page, identify what the audience should understand from it, what they must see, and what the speaker will supply. Use a meaningful takeaway as the title where appropriate; section dividers and orientation pages have different jobs. Check that the sequence connects, not merely that each page looks complete. A working outline or storyboard is useful when it resolves structure, not a mandatory deliverable.

For live delivery, let the screen carry salient evidence, relationships and anchors; let speaker notes carry explanation, examples and transitions. For standalone reading, keep essential context and reasoning visible without relying on an absent speaker. Synchronize any script, notes and alternate versions after changing the slide sequence.

Separate audience content, speaker guidance and production instructions by purpose. Do not leak drafting notes, internal labels, placeholders or private briefing details into delivered materials. Include background or an example identifier only when it helps the audience understand; do not ban useful content merely because it originated in the brief.

## Choose a visual form that explains

Choose diagrams for relationships, sequences for change, charts for quantities, and text or images where they communicate more directly. Do not force content into a template's empty cards. Make visual hierarchy follow the message; reuse a coherent visual language while varying composition to fit the information. Decoration should not compete with evidence.

Preserve meaningful chart scales and comparable quantities, label units, and make diagrams explain their arrows and relationships. Crop and annotate screenshots to expose the relevant detail without losing context. Check projection or reading conditions, legible labels, contrast and meaning beyond color. Reduce, reorganize or split overloaded content before shrinking type. Avoid universal word-count, slide-count or font-size formulas.

## Build with the available tools

Inspect actual authoring, rendering and export capabilities before choosing an implementation. Use the requested native format and retain meaningful editability; do not silently substitute an outline, renamed file or full-slide screenshot for an editable deck. Invoke format or diagram tools when needed, without making another skill, vendor or local path a mandatory dependency. Keep source changes bounded and do not upload private materials to an external service without authorization.

Test a representative page early when fonts, multilingual text, formulas, embedded media or export fidelity are uncertain. Use its rendered result to resolve implementation issues before multiplying them across a deck. Preserve the source and report a blocked export rather than disguising it as success.

## Verify the artifact and the delivery

Review the final sequence for missing premises, unsupported claims, repetition and material that does not serve the goal. Render the actual deliverable, inspect the whole-deck overview and every page at a readable size, and examine affected pages and shared-layout consumers again after repairs. Check glyphs, line breaks, clipping, overlap, contrast, chart labels, source attribution and slide-to-note correspondence. Test requested exports and relevant media or transitions separately; successful generation, text extraction and layout checks do not substitute for viewing the result.

Repair the cause: missing explanation needs content work, crowding needs selection or composition, glyph defects need font or rendering work, and overrun needs scope or pacing changes. Do not hide defects by deleting necessary meaning or rushing the speaker. Use a timed rehearsal when available; label estimates as estimates, and never claim the presenter rehearsed or the audience understood without that evidence.

Deliver the requested files and usable notes, not the internal production trail. State material limits precisely, separating content review, structural checks, rendered inspection, target-player testing and human rehearsal. Without a renderer, preserve useful native work but leave visual verification explicitly incomplete. Do not certify a deck from a screenshot sample or claim inspection of a file that was not opened.
