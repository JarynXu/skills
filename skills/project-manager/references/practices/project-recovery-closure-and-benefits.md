# Project recovery, closure, and benefits realization

Use this reference when the project is materially off-plan, confidence is low, a major reset is required, delivery is ending, or expected value continues after project closure.

## Recovery starts with facts, not rebranding

Do not begin by editing the schedule or status color. Establish:

- what has actually been delivered and accepted;
- current scope/requirements and unresolved change;
- current milestone/dependency state;
- actual cost/commitments and remaining funding;
- current team/vendor capacity;
- defects/quality/security/compliance state;
- critical risks/issues/assumptions;
- contract/regulatory deadlines;
- decision latency and governance constraints;
- operational/release readiness;
- which earlier assumptions are now false.

Freeze a factual snapshot before corrective actions make history harder to reconstruct.

## Stabilize first

For a severe project condition:

1. stop avoidable damage and new uncontrolled commitments;
2. protect critical delivery/production/customer obligations;
3. clarify decision authority and escalation cadence;
4. isolate the few constraints controlling outcomes;
5. preserve team focus by suspending nonessential reporting/initiatives;
6. communicate what is known, unknown and next decision point.

Do not “recover” by increasing overtime, adding people, compressing every estimate, or hiding scope.

## Reforecast from remaining work

Sunk history matters for learning/cost but does not determine future duration. Build a new forecast from:

```text
remaining accepted scope/outcomes
+ current actual state
+ remaining work and quality/transition obligations
+ current resources/capacity
+ current dependencies/constraints
+ current uncertainty/risk
```

Preserve the original baseline for variance/history. A recovery forecast is not automatically a new baseline.

## Develop options

Typical options include combinations of:

- reduce/defer/split scope;
- change release sequencing;
- add/reallocate capability;
- resolve vendor/procurement constraints;
- simplify architecture/integration with technical authority;
- change acceptance/migration strategy;
- extend date;
- increase budget;
- terminate/pause the project;
- convert to a different delivery model;
- renegotiate commercial commitments.

For each option state value, date, cost, resource, quality, risk, contract and operational consequences. Include confidence and irreversible decisions.

## Obtain explicit decisions

Recovery often crosses delegated tolerances. Present evidence and recommendation to the sponsor/governance authority. Record rejected options and why. Do not quietly rebaseline a project so the recovery plan appears “green.”

## Track a recovery plan

Use a small number of high-information controls:

- decisive milestones/outcomes;
- critical dependencies;
- top risks/issues;
- decision log and needed-by dates;
- quality/acceptance evidence;
- cost/resource forecast;
- weekly/appropriate recovery forecast and confidence;
- trigger for further escalation or stop decision.

## Closure criteria

Closure should verify:

- agreed deliverables accepted or formally dispositioned;
- product/service transitioned to named owner;
- open defects/risks/issues transferred with authority;
- procurement/contracts/invoices/claims closed or transferred;
- data/records/documentation archived as required;
- lessons captured in a reusable form;
- temporary environments/access/resources handled;
- team/resource release completed;
- remaining benefit measurement assigned;
- post-project obligations and review dates named.

Closing administration does not prove project success or benefit realization.

## Lessons learned

Capture causal, transferable lessons:

```text
context and decision
what was expected
what occurred
evidence/contributing mechanism
what to repeat/change
who will apply the lesson and where
```

Avoid blame narratives and generic advice such as “communicate more.” Preserve lessons throughout the project when evidence is fresh, then consolidate at closure.

## Benefits realization

Benefits often occur after the project ends. Define:

- benefit/outcome;
- owner;
- baseline;
- metric/evidence source;
- target/range and timing;
- disbenefits/negative outcomes;
- dependencies on adoption/operations/market conditions;
- measurement/review cadence;
- authority to respond if benefit is not realized.

Distinguish output from outcome: deploying a capability is an output; improved conversion, reduced handling time, compliance, resilience or strategic option value may be the benefit.

## Close with integrity

A project can close with incomplete benefits, transferred risks, accepted defects or changed scope if the right authorities explicitly disposition them. Integrity means the record explains what happened and who now owns what—not that every dashboard ends green.
